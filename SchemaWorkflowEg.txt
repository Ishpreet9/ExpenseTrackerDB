
This example demonstrates how all 7 tables connect and update over 

a few days for a single user, Rahul.



--------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1: SETUP THE PARENT TABLES

--------------------------------------------------------------------

Before Rahul can log any money, the lookup tables and his user 

profile must exist (because of the FOREIGN KEY rules).



[Users]

user_id | full_name    | email           | created_at

--------|--------------|-----------------|-----------

1       | Rahul Sharma | rahul@email.com | 01-NOV-26



[Income_Types]

type_id | type_name

--------|----------

1       | Salary

2       | Freelance



[Categories]

category_id | category_name

------------|--------------

1           | Food

2           | Rent





--------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 2: RAHUL GETS PAID (Income -> Savings goes UP)

--------------------------------------------------------------------

On Nov 1, Rahul receives his Rs. 50,000 salary. A row is inserted 

into Incomes linking user_id = 1 (Rahul) and type_id = 1 (Salary).



[Incomes]

income_id | user_id | type_id | title           | amount | income_date

----------|---------|---------|-----------------|--------|------------

101       | 1       | 1       | November Salary | 50000  | 01-NOV-26



Because of this income, Savings_History logs the change and sets 

his running balance to Rs. 50,000:



[Savings_History]

history_id | user_id | change_type | change_amount | current_savings | updated_at

-----------|---------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|-----------

1          | 1       | INCOME      | +50000        | 50000           | 01-NOV-26





--------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 3: RAHUL BUYS GROCERIES (Expense -> Savings goes DOWN)

--------------------------------------------------------------------

On Nov 3, Rahul spends Rs. 2,000 on groceries. A row is inserted 

into Expenses linking user_id = 1 (Rahul) and category_id = 1 (Food).



[Expenses]

expense_id | user_id | category_id | title            | amount | expense_date

-----------|---------|-------------|------------------|--------|-------------

201        | 1       | 1           | Weekly Groceries | 2000   | 03-NOV-26



Savings_History subtracts Rs. 2,000 from his previous balance 

(50,000 - 2,000):



[Savings_History] (new row added)

history_id | user_id | change_type | change_amount | current_savings | updated_at

-----------|---------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|-----------

1          | 1       | INCOME      | +50000        | 50000           | 01-NOV-26

2          | 1       | EXPENSE     | -2000         | 48000           | 03-NOV-26





--------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 4: RAHUL SCHEDULES A RECURRING BILL (Rent)

--------------------------------------------------------------------

Rahul pays Rs. 10,000 rent every month on the 5th. He saves it once 

in Recurring_Bills (is_active = 1 means it is turned ON).



[Recurring_Bills]

bill_id | user_id | category_id | title      | amount | frequency | next_due_date | is_active

--------|---------|-------------|------------|--------|-----------|---------------|----------

301     | 1       | 2           | House Rent | 10000  | MONTHLY   | 05-NOV-26     | 1



What happens on Nov 5 when the bill is due:

1. A new row is added to Expenses:

   (expense_id = 202, title = 'House Rent', amount = 10000)

2. Savings_History adds a 3rd row:

   (change_type = 'RECURRING_BILL', change_amount = -10000, current_savings = 38000)

3. In Recurring_Bills, next_due_date updates to '05-DEC-26' so it is 

   ready for next month.


--------------------------------------------------------------------

"Users, Income_Types, and Categories are our parent tables. Whenever 

a user logs an Income or Expense, it links to those IDs and 

automatically updates their running balance in Savings_History. 

Recurring_Bills stores repeating payments like rent, which feed into 

Expenses whenever their due date arrives."
